## The story in one paragraph

In November 1998 an unnamed reporter reads about a masked man in the woods near Leurs and travels there for three days. The villagers call him Le Loyon and laugh him off. The reporter learns about three graves on the hill: Daniel Menoud, who took his own life; Jean-Pierre Gremaud, the syndic who vanished; and Marc Chassot, a local man reported dead in Bosnia in 1995. Marc's coffin is empty. Inside it the syndic left a key and a letter. The key opens a sealed room in a disused army bunker in the forest, where Marc has lived for three years. He tells the reporter what happened and asks them to keep it to themselves. The reporter decides what to do with the story.

## Resolved story questions

### How Marc ended up in Bosnia

Marc Chassot was born in Leurs in 1958. In 1978, at 20, after his father's death and a row with the village, he went to Aubagne and joined the French Foreign Legion. He signed up under a declared identity, as every recruit did then: the Legion gave him the name **Marc Challand**, born in Belgium. The Legion lets a man regularise his real identity later. Marc never asked, because his real name led back to Switzerland, where serving in a foreign army is a crime. Over 17 years he served with the 2e REP (the parachute regiment at Calvi, Corsica) and rose to sergent-chef. He was in Sarajevo with the regiment's UN tour in 1993.

In summer 1995 he went back to Bosnia with the legionnaires sent to Mount Igman above Sarajevo as part of the UN's Rapid Reaction Force (UNPROFOR, the French call it FORPRONU). In August 1995 a mortar round hit the vehicle he was riding in on a mountain road outside the city. Two men died in the fire. One body could not be identified. Marc, burned on the left side of his face, neck, and hands, concussed and half deaf, walked away from the road in the dark and did not come back. The Legion listed sergent-chef Challand as missing, then as presumed dead.

The only civilian contact Marc had ever given the Legion was his godfather, Jean-Pierre Gremaud, the syndic of Leurs. The notice went to him. Gremaud told the village Marc was dead.

The details of the incident are fiction. No real event or real unit casualty is depicted. See [research.md](research.md) for what we checked and why the attack stays unnamed.

### Why the coffin is empty

Marc walked and hitched for nine weeks across Croatia, Slovenia, and Austria with no papers. He reached Leurs at the end of October 1995 and knocked on Gremaud's door at night.

He begged Gremaud to let him stay dead. He was a deserter from the Legion and, under Swiss military law, a criminal for having served in a foreign army at all. More than that, he could not stand to be seen. Gremaud agreed. He told the village the French authorities had sent home a sealed coffin with Marc's effects. In truth the coffin came from a carpenter in the next valley and held nothing. On Saturday 18 November 1995 the village buried it. Marc watched his own funeral from the trees above the cemetery.

Before the coffin was closed, Gremaud put a tin box inside it with the spare key to the bunker's inner room and a letter. Gremaud had a weak heart and knew it. Nobody but him knew where Marc was. The letter is addressed to whoever opens the coffin, on the reasoning that only someone who doubted Marc's death would ever dig, and that person might be the one to help him.

The pallbearers noticed the coffin was light. Nobody said so out loud.

### Why the bunker

The Leurs commune bought the old army fort in the Bois-Noir forest from the Confederation in 1994, when the army was giving up its older bunkers. It is a small infantry fort from the 1940s, disguised as a hay barn. The syndic held its keys. Gremaud hid Marc in the inner command room, which has a steel door and its own emergency exit, a short tunnel to a hatch in the forest. The commune never did anything with the place. Teenagers break into the outer rooms now and then to drink. They have never gotten past the steel door.

### The Gremaud subplot

For 14 months Gremaud carried food to the bunker two nights a week. His wife Odile thought he had a lover.

On the night of Tuesday 14 January 1997, in heavy snow, Gremaud collapsed on the forest path 200 m from the bunker. His heart gave out. Marc found him at first light, already dead, with the rucksack of bread and cheese still on his back.

Marc could not call anyone without giving himself up. He carried the body into the bunker. Snow covered the tracks. The village searched for five days, mostly toward the river and the road, because Gremaud's car was still in his garage and people assumed he had gone for a walk and fallen in. Searchers reached the bunker, found the barn door locked, and the commune's keys were missing. They were in Gremaud's coat pocket, inside.

In March 1997, when the ground thawed, Marc buried Gremaud under a larch in the forest above the bunker and marked the spot with stones and a wooden cross carved "J.-P. G. 14.I.1997". He kept the wedding ring to give back to Odile one day, and never found a way. In November 1997 the family put up a memorial stone in the cemetery, blessed on All Saints' Day.

This gives the player a second mystery and a real fear. On day 3 they find a hidden grave in the forest with the syndic's initials. It looks like Le Loyon killed him. Marc's monologue explains it. The player can never prove either version, and that weighs on the publish ending.

### Why he visits the graves

Each grave is a person he owes something.

- **Daniel Menoud** was his best friend from childhood. Daniel found Marc in the forest in spring 1996, knew him by his walk, and kept the secret. He came to the bunker on Sundays. Daniel had his own troubles: a failing farm, a divorce, depression nobody named. On Sunday 8 December 1996 he did not come. He died that day by suicide. Marc could not go to the funeral. Every Saturday night he lights the grave lantern on Daniel's grave. The village has never found out who does it.
- **Jean-Pierre Gremaud** died bringing him food. The stone in the cemetery stands over nothing, and only Marc knows where the man lies.
- **His own grave.** His mother Thérèse died in June 1996 believing her son was dead. She is buried in the Chassot family plot next to his empty coffin. He stands at her grave, which is also his. When a villager says Le Loyon "stares at a grave", it is this one.

Nothing in this is supernatural. Marc's PTSD shows in what he does: night walking, the mask, the routines, the startle at sound, the bunker, the inability to face people.

### Why the mask

His face and neck carry burn scars on the left side. They are not as bad as the village rumors say. He wears an old tan rubber army gas mask from the bunker's own stores because with it on, people look at the mask and not at him, and because inside it he smells only rubber and charcoal. The cape is a Swiss army tent sheet in the old 1950s camouflage, also from the bunker. Le Loyon is a costume made of whatever the fort had in its cupboards.

### Who "veilleur" is

A forum user called **veilleur** ("watchman") posts during the game and always seems to know where the reporter is. It is **Yannick Rossier**, 16, the innkeeper's son, apprentice mechanic at the garage. He uses the inn's computer at night, watches the square from the upstairs window, and follows the reporter at night. He has seen Le Loyon himself, several times, and is a little in love with the mystery. He also signed the guestbook in the prologue as "yann". The player finds out on the evening of day 3 (see [07-scares.md](07-scares.md), jumpscare 2). Until then the posts should feel like they come from nowhere.

### Ambiguity until the end

The game keeps three doubts open: whether Le Loyon is a man at all, whether he killed Gremaud, and who is watching the reporter. Rules for every writer and level builder:

- Never show Le Loyon's face or a clear full-body view before the hidden room.
- His movement in sightings is slow and stiff. He limps on the left leg, which reads as "wrong" at a distance.
- Villagers never say he is a ghost. They say he is a joke, a drunk, a tramp, or nothing. The doubt comes from what the player sees.
- One Polaroid (photo 7, see [06-documents.md](06-documents.md)) shows a figure the player did not see. In the hidden room the player learns Marc was there and saw the flash.

## The map of Leurs

Leurs is a farming village of about 300 people at around 750 m, on a low ridge of the Fribourg plateau. In November it sits inside the stratus: gray all day, a few meters of visibility, wet ground. The village lies on one road. The church and square are at the center. The cemetery is on a hill north of the village, and the Bois-Noir forest covers the slope behind it.

The reporter has no name and no gender. Nobody says monsieur or madame to them. People use "you", "the journalist", "the stranger", or nothing. Monique calls the reporter by their room: "number three". In French lines, use forms that do not mark gender ("Bonsoir", "Vous êtes journaliste ?").

## Flow of play

Target times are real play time for a first run. Total about 2 hours.

### Prologue: Monday 16 November 1998, 23:10 (10 to 15 min)

Area: `home`. Full text in [01-prologue.md](01-prologue.md).

The reporter sits at their home PC. An email from the editor gives them three days for a "strange Switzerland" piece. They dial up, read the Alpine Anomalies message board and Stéphane's homepage with blurry photos. His guestbook is optional reading.

**Puzzle P1: find the village.** Kept short on purpose after playtesting: everything is in one thread. In the GAS MASK MAN thread, Cloudy's reply links straight to Stéphane's Le Loyon page, which names Leurs in the text and in a photo caption. pivex's reply in the same thread gives the PostBus line and "4 buses a day, the last at 18:52". When the player has read both, the reporter's notes file `notes.txt` fills in: "Leurs (FR). PostBus. Inn: Croix-Blanche." The Shut Down option then becomes "Pack and go".

Flags set: `pro_read_mail`, `pro_read_board`, `pro_read_thread_bus`, `pro_read_homepage`, `pro_knows_village`, `pro_done`, and `pro_read_guestbook` if the player opens the guestbook.

Items gained at the end of the prologue: `notebook`, `polaroid_camera` with 10 shots (`film_shots` counter), `pocket_money` (flavor only).

### Day 1: Tuesday 17 November, 16:20 to evening (25 min)

Areas open: `village_square`, `inn`, `church`, `laiterie`, `garage_dousse`, `yerly_farm`, `house_gremaud` (doorstep only), `chassot_farm` (exterior), `cemetery_path`, `cemetery`.
Closed: the forest past the cemetery ("I'd lose myself in this fog in a minute."), the bunker.

1. The PostBus drops the reporter in the square. The driver gives the last bus time. Sets `arrived`.
2. **Check in** at the inn with Monique. Gain `inn_room_key`. Set `inn_checked_in`.
3. **The abbé.** At the church, Abbé Overney tells the stories of the three graves. Set `abbe_met`, `abbe_told_graves`. He mentions the register but says to come back after morning mass.
4. Talk to anyone else. Gilles says Le Loyon was seen "staring at a grave". Bernard is short with the reporter and worried about a heifer that got out (`heifer_missing`). Christine at the laiterie mentions the lantern on her brother's grave. Odile shuts the door.
5. **The inn computer** in the stübli. Forum stage 1 posts (see [02-village.md](02-village.md)).
6. **Dinner.** When `abbe_told_graves` is set and the reporter talks to Monique after 18:00 (after any two other conversations), she serves soup and, if asked about walking at night, lends a torch. Gain `flashlight`. Set `dinner_day1`. This starts night 1.

### Night 1 (20 min)

Set `night = 1`. Outdoor areas switch to night. The village is dark, one window lit at the inn upstairs (Yannick).

1. **Cemetery by torchlight.** Read the three graves: `read_grave_menoud`, `read_grave_gremaud`, `read_grave_chassot`. The lantern on Daniel's grave is lit, but nobody is around: `saw_lantern_lit`.
2. **Meet the watchman.** Raymond Bapst and his dog Bari come up the path on their round. The dog barks before you see them (jumpscare 1, see [07-scares.md](07-scares.md)). Raymond tells the reporter the cemetery is no place at night. Set `met_raymond`. This teaches the patrol: light, bell on the dog's collar, whistling.
3. **First sighting.** When all three graves are read and Raymond has gone back down, a figure stands at the back wall against the forest, by the Chassot plot. If the player walks toward it, it turns and goes through the back gate. Set `first_sighting`. The forest path opens.
4. **Follow it.** The forest at night. The figure is seen twice more, far ahead, then lost at the shrine fork. The path leads to the barn. The wooden door opens, the steel door behind it will not: something holds it from inside. Set `bunker_door_wedged`, `found_bunker`. A metal sound inside, then silence.
5. Back at the inn. Sleeping in room 3 ends night 1.

### Day 2: Wednesday 18 November (35 min)

Morning. Forum stage 2.

Areas newly open: `forest_path` by day, `bunker_exterior`, `bunker_interior`. Gremaud house kitchen if `read_register` is set.

**Puzzle P2: the register.** After morning mass the abbé lets the reporter into the sacristy to read the register (`read_register`). The player must find three entries and notice what is odd in Marc's: "Cercueil scellé remis par M. le syndic J.-P. Gremaud. Dépouille non présentée." The abbé will not say more and makes clear he cannot. Set `abbe_seal_hint`. Full text in [03-church.md](03-church.md).

**Puzzle P3: the heifer and the shovel.** Bernard's heifer Marquise got out in the fog (`heifer_missing`, set on day 1). The player finds her at the forest edge above the Yerly field, standing still and staring into the trees (a sighting beat). Walk up to her and she follows the reporter back to the farm. Bernard thanks them, tells the story of the light coffin (`bernard_light_coffin`), and lends the shovel without asking what for. Gain `shovel`. Flags `heifer_found`, `heifer_returned`.

If the player asks for the shovel before the heifer is returned, Bernard refuses.

**Puzzle P4: the hidden door.** In daylight the steel door of the bunker stands open. Whatever held it is gone (`bunker_door_open`). The outer rooms have beer cans, graffiti, old army gear, and the room of gas masks. In the dormitory, a tall steel cupboard stands against the end wall. The dust on the floor in front of it is swept in a quarter circle. Push the cupboard (it moves on its own rollers if the player has looked at the floor first) to find a steel door with a stamped plate "K.P. 3" and a keyhole. Locked. Set `found_hidden_door`. A Swiss army biscuit wrapper nearby is new: `found_biscuit_wrapper`.

**Other day 2 conversations:**

- Gilles gives the 1979 postcard from Calvi (`postcard_calvi`), the first hard clue about the Legion. Set `knows_legion`.
- Odile, if `read_register` is set, lets the reporter in. She talks about Jean-Pierre's night walks in 1996 and shows his diary (`gremaud_diary`). Set `odile_talked`.
- Christine talks about Daniel: "He said once Marc wasn't dead. Then he laughed." Set `christine_daniel_hint`.

**Suspicion.** `suspects_empty_coffin` is set when the player has at least two of: `read_register`, `bernard_light_coffin`, `odile_talked`. The reporter's notebook then reads: "Marc Chassot's coffin. The syndic handed it over. It was light. What was in it?"

Evening: forum stage 3. Dinner with Monique (`dinner_day2`) starts night 2. Requires `has_shovel` and `suspects_empty_coffin`; otherwise the reporter says "Not yet. I need to know more first." when trying to go to bed, and the player keeps exploring day 2.

### Night 2: the dig (20 min)

Set `night = 2`. Full text in [04-cemetery.md](04-cemetery.md).

**Puzzle P5: the dig.** The player digs while avoiding Raymond's rounds.

- Digging works only on the Chassot grave (Marc's side of the plot) and the Gremaud stone.
- Daniel's grave: the reporter refuses. "No. Not him." No shovel action.
- Thérèse Chassot's side: the reporter refuses. "That's his mother."
- Gremaud stone: two strokes hit a concrete footing. "There's nothing under this. There never was." Noise raises the patrol's alert (Raymond comes one round early).
- Chassot grave: five digging stages. Each takes about 25 s of holding the action button. Raymond's round comes about every 90 s. The dog's collar bell is audible 15 s before his torch reaches the gate. The player must switch off the flashlight and crouch behind a stone or in the ossuary shed until he passes.
- If Raymond's torch beam touches the reporter while they are standing in or by the open grave, or while their own flashlight is on inside the cemetery: game over, `caught`. See the watchman text.

At stage five the shovel hits wood. The lid is loose. The coffin is empty except for a gray metal box. Inside: `bunker_key` (stamped "K.P. 3"), `gremaud_letter`, and `legion_tag`, Marc's Legion identity disc in the name CHALLAND. Set `opened_coffin`, `has_bunker_key`, `read_gremaud_letter`.

**Optional: refill.** Three stages, same rules. Set `grave_refilled`. If the player leaves without refilling, set `grave_left_open`. Raymond finds it at dawn.

Sleep ends night 2.

### Day 3: Thursday 19 November (25 min)

Morning. Forum stage 4. Village reactions depend on `grave_left_open` (see [02-village.md](02-village.md)). If the grave was left open, Raymond has called the gendarmerie, who will come Friday. The village is cold to the stranger. Monique asks the reporter to leave on Friday's first bus.

Areas newly open: `forest_deep`.

1. **The forest grave.** The Gremaud letter mentions "the larch above the fort". By day, the forest above the bunker leads to a larch with a mound of stones and a cross carved "J.-P. G. 14.I.1997". Set `found_forest_grave`. Horror beat: it reads as murder.
2. **The editor calls** the inn phone at noon. "Saturday's edition. What have you got?" Set `editor_called`.
3. **Food.** The letter says to bring food. Christine sells bread and Gruyère, or Monique packs some if asked. Gain `food_parcel` (optional, changes Marc's mood).
4. **The abbé, day 3.** If the player has the letter, he answers only "Jean-Pierre came to see me often that last year. What he told me, I keep. Bring him bread." Set `abbe_day3`.
5. Optional: show Odile the cross? No. The reporter will not tell her yet. The game blocks it with "Not before I know."
6. Evening at the inn computer: forum stage 5, then jumpscare 2, Yannick in the doorway. He admits to being veilleur. Set `met_veilleur`. This conversation is required to start night 3, so it always happens: the evening computer visit is the trigger.

### Night 3: the bunker (20 min)

Set `night = 3`. Full text in [05-forest-bunker.md](05-forest-bunker.md).

**Puzzle P6: three knocks.** Gremaud's letter says: "Knock three times, wait, then once. He will know it is a friend." The player reaches the hidden door with `bunker_key`.

- If the player uses the knock action in the right pattern (`knock_pattern_done`): a voice answers through the door after a long pause. The door opens from inside. Marc stands back in the dark, masked. Calmer meeting.
- If the player just unlocks the door: the room is empty and lit by one candle. The player explores Marc's things. When they turn to leave, Marc stands in the doorway behind them (jumpscare 3). He does not move toward them.

Either way, in the room the player can read: Marc's notebook with drawings of the reporter at the cemetery (`read_marc_notebook`), the photo of two boys (`photo_boys`), the Legion képi blanc, Gremaud's ring in a tin with a note (`ring_note`).

**Marc's monologue.** Choices in the conversation raise `marc_trust` (0 to 5). Asking to photograph him: if `marc_trust >= 2`, he lets the reporter photograph the mask only (`photo_marc_masked`). If not, the reporter can take the picture anyway (`photo_marc_forced`), and he turns away and the scene ends coldly.

At the end of the monologue Marc asks the reporter to keep the secret and gives the ring to return to Odile if they will (`has_ring`, only if `marc_trust >= 1`). The reporter leaves through the escape tunnel at dawn.

### Endings: Friday 20 November

Back in room 3 at dawn. A local reporter in 1998 has no laptop. They write the piece longhand in the notebook and phone the copy in, as reporters did. The final choice is made at the phone in the inn's stübli:

- **Phone in the story** → `ending_publish`.
- **Tell the editor there's nothing** → `ending_secret`.

The third ending happens earlier:

- **Leave** → `ending_leave`. Available at the bus stop any time from day 1 evening until `met_marc` is set. Confirm prompt: "Take the bus and go home?" Also forced if the player tries to stay a fourth night without `met_marc` (not reachable in normal play, since nights advance by story).

`caught` is a failure state, not an ending. It shows a short text and a "Continue from the last sleep" option.

Full ending text is in [05-forest-bunker.md](05-forest-bunker.md).

## Dialogue convention

Every dialogue in this folder uses the format below. A programmer can turn it into TypeScript data line by line.

```
### node_id
@speaker: speaker_id
?if: flag_a, !flag_b, marc_trust >= 2
! set: flag_c, marc_trust += 1, give: item_id, take: item_id

> First line of dialogue.
> Second line.
- [Choice text] -> other_node_id
- [Choice text] ?if: flag_d -> node_three ! set: flag_e
-> next_node_id
```

Rules:

1. **Node.** A level-3 heading `### node_id` starts a node. Only headings whose whole text matches `^[a-z0-9_]+$` are nodes; headings with spaces or capitals ("### Entry", "### The round") are structure for human readers. Ids are snake_case and unique across the whole script. Prefix them with the character id: `monique_checkin`, `overney_graves_1`.
2. **Speaker.** `@speaker:` takes a character id from the cast table, or `reporter` for the player character's own spoken lines, or `narration` for description and inner thoughts (rendered in italics, no name). It applies to every line in the node until another `@speaker:` line appears inside the node.
3. **Condition.** `?if:` is a comma-separated list, all of which must be true (logical AND). `!flag` means the flag is false. Numeric comparisons use `>=`, `<=`, `==`, `!=`. A node without `?if:` is always available.
4. **Effects.** `! set:` runs when the node starts. Items: `flag_name` sets true, `flag_name = false` clears it, `counter += n`, `counter = n`, `give: item_id`, `take: item_id`.
5. **Lines.** Each `>` line is one dialogue box. Keep each under 120 characters, not counting a `{{translation}}`, which shows on its own line.
6. **Choices.** `- [text] -> target`. A choice can carry its own `?if:` (hidden when false) and its own `! set:` (runs when picked), written after the text and before or after the arrow, as shown.
7. **Jump.** A line `-> target` with no choice text continues automatically. A jump can carry a condition, `-> target ?if: flag_a`. A node may list several jumps; the first whose condition holds is taken. A node with no lines and only jumps is a router. A node with neither choices nor a jump ends the conversation.
8. **Entry points.** Under each character, a table called "Entry" lists which node opens when the player talks to them, checked top to bottom. The first row whose condition holds wins.
9. **French.** French phrases sit inline in the line. A translation for the subtitle tooltip, if needed, follows in `{{ }}`: `> Bonsoir. {{Good evening.}}`. The game shows the English in a smaller line under the box.
10. **Notes in prose.** Any line that does not start with `@`, `?if:`, `! set:`, `>`, `- [` or `->` is a note for humans: descriptions, stage directions, timing. The converter skips it, inside or outside nodes.
11. **Display names.** `marc` shows as "Le Loyon" until `marc_named` is set, then "Marc". `reporter` lines show with no name label. `narration` lines show in italics with no label.

Documents (letters, inscriptions, posts) use a different format, given at the top of [06-documents.md](06-documents.md).

Example:

```
### bernard_shovel_ask
@speaker: reporter
?if: !heifer_returned

> Could I borrow a shovel?

@speaker: bernard
> A shovel. For what?
> No. Not with that cow still out there. Ask me when she's home.
```
