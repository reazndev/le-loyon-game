## The Foreign Legion's declared identity

**What we checked.** Whether a recruit in the 1970s could join under a false name, and whether he would keep it.

**What sources say.**

- The Legion allows enlistment under a declared identity (identité déclarée) when the recruit can't or doesn't provide documents. The declared identity counts as his military identity until the minister completes the regularisation of his military status (RSM). After regularisation, service under the declared identity is recognised under the real name. ([Assemblée nationale, written question QANR5L13QE71243](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/13/questions/QANR5L13QE71243.pdf); [Assemblée nationale, QANR5L13QE70012](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/13/questions/QANR5L13QE70012.pdf))
- The ministry describes the procedure as originally meant for wartime enlistment, and now used in peacetime to help young men break with a painful past. ([Assemblée nationale, QANR5L11QE29133](https://questions.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/11/questions/QANR5L11QE29133.pdf))
- A legionnaire needs regularisation before he can marry, which shows that regularisation was a step men took when they needed it. ([Assemblée nationale, QANR5L13QE85622](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/13/questions/QANR5L13QE85622.pdf))
- Recruitment background: [Theatrum Belli, le recrutement à la Légion étrangère](https://theatrum-belli.com/le-recrutement-a-la-legion-etrangere/?pdf=325014).

**In the script.** Marc enlists in 1978 as "Marc Challand", declared Belgian, and never applies for regularisation, because his real name would tie him to Switzerland and to art. 94. His Legion identity disc in the coffin reads CHALLAND. The disc's number and format are invented.

**Not verified.** How long men in the 1980s and 90s typically stayed under a declared identity, and exactly who the Legion would notify after a death or disappearance. The script has the Legion notify the one civilian contact Marc gave, Gremaud. Treat this as dramatic license.

## The Legion and UNPROFOR in Bosnia, 1992 to 1995

**What sources say.**

- From 5 June 1992 UNPROFOR was responsible for Sarajevo airport. The French contingent was mainly in Sarajevo and held the airport. ([Wikipedia, United Nations Protection Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Protection_Force))
- About 450 legionnaires of the 2e REP prepared to join the UN force in Sarajevo at the end of December 1992. They were relieved by the 2e REI. A 1995 photo shows the 2e REI's colour party at a decoration ceremony in Bosnia. ([Chemins de mémoire, 2e REP Sarajevo, PDF](https://www.cheminsdememoire.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/2019-06/2e%20REP%20Sarajevo.pdf))
- The 2e REP's Sarajevo mission ran from December 1992 to July 1993; the legionnaires wore blue berets and were not allowed to use their weapons. ([foreignlegion.info, 2e REP Sarajevo 1993](https://foreignlegion.info/2019/08/20/2-rep-sarajevo-in-yugoslavia-1993/))
- The Rapid Reaction Force was approved in June 1995, about ten thousand men, mainly French, British, and Dutch. ([Universalis, 2 to 30 June 1995](https://www.universalis.fr/evenement/2-30-juin-1995-liberation-des-casques-bleus-creation-de-la-force-de-reaction-rapide-et-reprise-des-combats/))
- On its first deployment, British troops joined "500 French legionnaires" on top of Mount Igman overlooking Sarajevo. ([Macau Daily Times, "This day in history", BBC report](https://macaudailytimes.com.mo/?p=182467)); context in ([Christian Science Monitor, 4 August 1995](https://www.csmonitor.com/layout/set/amphtml/1995/0804/04061.html)).
- 2e REP history: based at Calvi, Corsica since 1967; Chad, Kolwezi (1978), Lebanon (1982), Rwanda (1990, 1992), Gulf (1991). ([foreignlegion.info, 2e REP history](https://foreignlegion.info/2014/02/09/2rep-history-last-foreign-legion-airborne-unit/); [Flags of the World, 2e REP](https://crwflags.com/fotw/Flags/fr^lege2.html); [Grey Dynamics, 2e REP](https://greydynamics.com/2e-rep-2nd-foreign-parachute-regiment/))

**In the script.** Marc served with the 2e REP. His postcard of 1979 comes from Calvi. He lists "Chad, Lebanon, the Gulf, Sarajevo in '93, then Sarajevo again." In 1995 he is with the Legion on Mount Igman. We don't name which Legion unit sent the Igman detachment, because our sources don't say.

**Not verified.** Any Legion vehicle casualties on Igman. The attack in the script is fiction and never named or dated more exactly than "August 1995".

## Swiss Military Criminal Code, art. 94: foreign military service

**What sources say.**

- Art. 94 para. 1: "Tout Suisse qui, sans l'autorisation du Conseil fédéral, aura pris du service dans une armée étrangère, sera puni d'une peine privative de liberté de trois ans au plus ou d'une peine pécuniaire." Para. 2 exempts dual nationals settled in the other state. The article's wording dates from a law of 21 December 1950, in force 1 July 1951. ([Code pénal militaire, consolidated text, via lexfind.ch, PDF](https://lexfind.ch/tolv/140329/fr))
- The Military Court of Cassation treats it as an abstract endangerment offence that protects national defence, foreign relations, and neutrality. ([UniDistance, Thierry Godel's book on the prohibition](https://unidistance.ch/en/law/news/the-prohibition-for-swiss-nationals-to-serve-in-foreign-armed-forces-professor-thierry-godels-new-book-is-published-on-weblaw); [UniDistance research project](https://unidistance.ch/en/law/research-project/neutral-switzerlands-participation-in-armed-conflicts-abroad-legal-prohibitions-and-obligations))
- A French parliamentary report notes that Switzerland stands out in Europe for punishing individual service in a foreign army. ([Assemblée nationale, report 671](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/12/pdf/rapports/r0671.pdf))

**In the script.** Gilles: "A Swiss in a foreign army, that's prison." Marc: "The French would want me for leaving. The Swiss would want me for going." The publish ending mentions the military justice office opening a file.

**Note.** The text we read is a 2006 consolidation. The "peine pécuniaire" wording came with the reform of the general part of criminal law in 2007; in 1998 the penalty would have been written as imprisonment. We did not find the 1998 wording itself. The script never quotes the article, so nothing depends on it.

## Swiss Catholic parish life in Fribourg

**What sources say.**

- The canton of Fribourg belongs to the Diocese of Lausanne, Geneva and Fribourg, with the bishop's seat in Fribourg. ([religion.info, 2014](https://religion.info/2014/02/27/suisse-pratique-religieuse-catholiques-fribourg))
- Catholic parishes in Fribourg are organised as ecclesiastical corporations under cantonal law of 1990; parish archives are being inventoried by the canton. ([État de Fribourg, recensement du patrimoine des paroisses](https://www.fr.ch/dfac/sbc/biens-culturels-meubles/projet-recensement-du-patrimoine-des-paroisses))
- Swiss French Catholic usage writes a priest as "M. l'abbé" with his office (curé, vicaire) given separately: a 1956 letter is addressed "Monsieur l'Abbé ... Curé de la paroisse Saint-Joseph" and opens "Cher Monsieur le Curé". ([Échos de Saint-Maurice, archive](https://www.aasm.ch/pages/echos/ESM104005.pdf))
- The seal of confession is inviolable (canon 983); the confessor may not betray the penitent "by word or in any manner", and may not use knowledge from confession to the penitent's detriment even without risk of revealing it (canon 984). Direct violation brings automatic excommunication (canon 1388). ([Simply Catholic, the seal of confession](https://www.simplycatholic.com/the-seal-of-confession/); [National Catholic Register](https://www.ncregister.com/blog/some-answers-about-the-seal-of-confession); [Sydney Catholic, can a priest break the seal](https://library.sydneycatholic.org/?p=243))

**In the script.** Abbé Overney is the curé of Leurs; villagers call him "Monsieur l'abbé" and "the abbé". Gremaud confessed to him in November 1996 (the diary says "Confession", 12 November). The abbé never reveals it. He points the reporter only at the register, which he wrote in 1995 before the confession, and he lets Odile decide whether to talk. His "Bring him bread" on day 3 is the closest he comes to the line, and he says no name. The burial register, baptism register, and parish chronicle follow the usual Catholic set of registers; their layout is our design.

**Note.** A suicide receiving a full church funeral: the 1983 Code of Canon Law dropped the old bar on church funerals for suicides. We did not search for a source on this; the abbé's note in the register and the old villagers' objection reflect the change of attitude, and nothing in the plot depends on it.

## The syndic

**What sources say.**

- In the cantons of Fribourg and Vaud, the head of the commune's executive is the syndic (syndique for a woman). The executive is the conseil communal, led by the syndic. ([Historical Dictionary of Switzerland, HLS](https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/fr/articles/027477/))
- Fribourg's communes law sets three organs: the electorate, the communal assembly or general council, and the communal council. ([Loi sur les communes, via lexfind.ch](https://www.lexfind.ch/tolv/24308/fr))

**In the script.** Jean-Pierre Gremaud was syndic of Leurs from 1971 to 1997. He holds the keys to commune property, including the fort, and handles the commune's dealings, which is how he could take delivery of a coffin "from the French" without questions. Re-election over 26 years in a village of 300 is plausible for a small commune but is our invention.

## 1998: phones and dial-up

**What sources say.**

- Telecom PTT launched The Blue Window on 16 September 1996, with local-rate access across Switzerland and a 25-franc monthly fee for 20 hours. It became Switzerland's leading internet provider. It was renamed bluewin in 2000. ([bluewin, "the day the internet arrived"](https://www.bluewin.ch/en/digital/the-day-the-internet-arrived-li.3523553); [bluewin, from the Blue Window to blue News](https://www.bluewin.ch/en/news/from-the-blue-window-to-blue-news-a-story-like-no-other-li.3585121); [Swisscom history](https://swisscom.com/en/about/company/history.html))
- Telecom PTT became Swisscom on 1 October 1997. The IPO followed in early October 1998 (one source dates it 5 October 1998). ([Wikipedia, Swisscom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swisscom); [Euromoney, best IPOs](https://www.euromoney.com/article/27bjsstsqxhkmh14o49av/banking/best-ipos-selling-telecoms-in-a-storm/); [RCR Wireless, 5 October 1998](https://www.rcrwireless.com/19981005/archived-articles/swisscom-ipo-expected-soon))
- Natel D, the GSM network, launched in 1993 per Wikipedia and Blick, or 1992 per HLS. ([Wikipedia, Natel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natel); [HLS](https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/fr/articles/027832))
- The Taxcard was the Telecom PTT phone card of the mid-90s. ([Fitzwilliam Museum, Taxcard](https://collection.beta.fitz.ms/id/object/277715))

**In the script.** The home PC dials an invented provider, Helvenet, at 33,600 bps; the inn's older machine has a 28.8k modem. Being online ties up the phone line, which Monique complains about. The portal headline "Swisscom: first weeks on the stock market" fits mid-November 1998. The village phone box takes Taxcards. Nobody in Leurs has a mobile phone on screen: Natel D existed, but a village of farmers in 1998 is a fair place not to have one, and the reporter's paper wouldn't have paid for one. We use an invented provider to keep a real brand out of the game.

## PostBus

**What sources say.**

- All PostBuses have been the same yellow since 1959. ([PostBus, history](https://www.postauto.ch/en/history-postbus))
- The three-tone horn dates from 1924 and is used only on mountain postal roads, marked with a yellow horn on blue. ([PostBus, 100 years of the post horn](https://www.postauto.ch/en/about-us-and-news/organization/100-years-of-the-post-horn); [Wikipedia, PostBus Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PostBus_Switzerland))
- PostBus ran sparse rural routes and in 1995 started PubliCar on-demand service for thinly populated areas. ([PostBus, history](https://www.postauto.ch/en/history-postbus))

**In the script.** A yellow PostBus, four runs a day, terminus in the village square. No horn. The driver's lines and timetable are invented.

## November fog on the Swiss plateau

**What sources say.**

- November and December are the typical months for fog or low stratus that lasts all day on the plateau. It comes with high pressure: cold air pools under a temperature inversion and weak winter sun can't break it. Only stronger wind, usually from a passing cold front, clears it. ([MeteoSwiss, fog in autumn](https://www.meteoswiss.admin.ch/weather/weather-and-climate-from-a-to-z/fog-in-autumn.html); [MeteoSwiss, Swiss plateau fog hole](https://www.meteoswiss.admin.ch/weather/weather-and-climate-from-a-to-z/fog/swiss-plateau-fog-hole.html))
- With a bise (north-easterly) the fog top is generally above 1,000 m; without a pressure gradient it sinks to about 600 to 800 m. Above it the sky is clear. ([MeteoSwiss, bise](https://www.meteoswiss.admin.ch/weather/weather-and-climate-from-a-to-z/bise.html); [MeteoSwiss technical report 266](https://www.meteoschweiz.admin.ch/dam/jcr:a06df83d-2809-409b-be10-69729b670594/meteoSchweiz_fachbericht-266.pdf))
- The weather office was the Swiss Meteorological Institute (SMA) from 1979. Wikipedia dates the MeteoSwiss name to 1996, HLS to 2000. ([Wikipedia, MeteoSwiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MeteoSwiss); [HLS](https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/fr/articles/024475))

**In the script.** Leurs is at 760 m. The prologue bulletin gives a stratus top of 900 to 1,100 m with a weak bise, so the village sits inside the fog all week. The PostBus driver says it will go "when the bise comes", which is half right: a strong bise or a front would clear it. Locals often say it that way, and the driver isn't a meteorologist.

## Decommissioned Swiss army bunkers

**What sources say.**

- The army has sold off most of its decommissioned fortifications; about a thousand unused bunkers remained in 2016, many still disguised as houses and barns. Former forts have become hotels, data centres, museums, and cheese stores. ([PRI, "picturesque barn ... former army bunker"](https://www.pri.org/stories/2016/01/09/picturesque-barn-swiss-countryside-former-army-bunker); [The Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/news/world/old-military-bunkers-reinvented-for-commercial-use/article28075973))
- The army began decommissioning its WWII-era forts in 1995. Another account says 13,000 of 20,000 installations were declassified in the late 1980s and early 1990s. ([Last Stand on Zombie Island, Reuters photo essay](https://laststandonzombieisland.com/2016/01/26/remember-that-747-full-of-cruise-missiles-hey/the-wider-image-a-new-life-for-swiss-army-bunkers-2/); [Newly Swissed, inside a secret bunker](https://www.newlyswissed.com/inside-a-secret-military-bunker-in-switzerland))
- WWII bunkers were dressed as chalets and farm buildings. Theatre set designers worked on them, and they had to fool the eye from at least 20 m. ([Messy Nessy Chic, fake chalets](https://www.messynessychic.com/2015/06/26/fake-chalets-unmasking-the-bunkers-disguised-as-quaint-swiss-villas/); [swissinfo](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/swiss-politics/swiss-army-targets-tourists/1022282))
- Example: Fuchsegg fortress, built 1943, in military use until 1993, with a machine-gun bunker disguised as a stable. ([Messy Nessy Chic](https://www.messynessychic.com/2015/06/26/fake-chalets-unmasking-the-bunkers-disguised-as-quaint-swiss-villas/))

**In the script.** The Bois-Noir fort is a 1940s infantry fort disguised as a hay barn. Its last army inspection was 1993 and the commune bought it from the Confederation in 1994. Its plate reads "Ouvrage A 3417"; the number is invented. "K.P." for the command room stands for Kommandoposten; we use it because Swiss army signs are bilingual German and French, but we did not find a source for the abbreviation on fort doors. Signs like "Gasschleuse / Sas à gaz" follow the same bilingual habit. The pile of old gas masks is our invention, taken from the reference image.

The tent-sheet cape: the Swiss army used its 1950s camouflage, modeled on the German Leibermuster, on shelter halves (Zeltbahn) and other items, and an ochre poncho in that pattern was in service into the 1990s. ([camopedia, Switzerland](https://camopedia.org/index.php/Switzerland); [Wikipedia, TAZ 83](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAZ_83)) A stock of these in an old fort is plausible.

## Smaller checks

- **Bière.** The Bière training ground in Vaud has trained artillery and infantry since 1874 and is the second-largest in Switzerland. Gilles's "recruit school together, summer '77, in Bière, artillery" fits. We did not confirm which recruit schools ran there in 1977. ([HLS, Bière](https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/fr/articles/002295); [Le Temps](https://www.letemps.ch/suisse/lattente-dune-incertaine-reprise-activites-militaires-biere-se-sent-desarmee))
- **Dates.** 1 November 1998 was a Sunday, so Monday 16 November is the prologue and Tuesday 17 to Friday 20 is the game. 18 November 1995, the funeral, was a Saturday. 14 January 1997 was a Tuesday, matching Gremaud's Tuesday and Friday deliveries. 8 December 1996 was a Sunday, matching Daniel's Sunday visits. Checked with the Unix `date` command.
- **Grave clearance.** Swiss cemeteries clear and reuse graves after a fixed term, commonly 20 to 25 years, which is why the script's stones date from the 1970s on and old stones are fixed to the wall. This is general knowledge we did not source; the exact term varies by commune.
- **Death notice format.** "Cet avis tient lieu de lettre de faire-part" is the standard closing of Swiss French death notices. General knowledge, not sourced.

## Kept fictional on purpose

- **Leurs**, its families, and its fort are invented. The village is placed only as "canton of Fribourg".
- **Marc Chassot** is not based on any real person. Nothing in the script refers to any real individual connected with the Le Loyon sightings in western Switzerland, and the script makes no claims about them.
- The newspapers *L'Écho* and *Le Journal du Plateau*, the provider Helvenet, the board Alpine Anomalies, and the host netcity.ch are invented.
- The Bosnian incident and Marc's Legion identity number are invented.
