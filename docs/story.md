## Decided

- **Name.** The legend is called **Le Loyon**.
- **Village.** **Leurs**, an invented village in the canton of Fribourg.
- **Era.** November 1998. The fog over the Fribourg plateau needs no explanation.
- **Player.** An unnamed local news reporter from somewhere else in Switzerland. Nobody uses a name or a gender for them.
- **Prologue.** The player browses late-90s web forums at home and first reads about Le Loyon there.
- **Forum during the game.** The inn has a computer with a dial-up modem.
- **Le Loyon.** A real man in a gas mask and a camouflage cape. Nothing supernatural.
- **Grave digging.** Digging is a crime. Getting caught by the watchman ends the night.
- **Endings.** Keep the secret, publish, or leave.
- **Single player.**

## Premise

Late-90s forums carry posts about a man in a gas mask and a long camouflage cape seen near the cemetery of Leurs and in the Bois-Noir forest behind it. People say you hear him before you see him, a slow rasping breath in the fog. A year ago a homemade gravestone appeared in the cemetery overnight. It says ROSE, 1962 to 1971, and under that JE TE CHERCHE, "I am looking for you". Nobody in Leurs knows a Rose. The reporter comes to find out who the masked man is. What they find is a child the village decided to forget.

## Le Loyon

**Lucien Bielmann**, 39, born in the poor lower town of Fribourg. In 1966 the guardianship office placed him, aged 7, and his sister **Rose**, aged 3, on the Pittet farm in Leurs as *enfants placés*, foster children who worked for their keep. Switzerland placed tens of thousands of children this way until the 1970s. Many were used as farm labor and some were beaten. The state apologized in 2013.

In January 1971 Rose, 8, fell ill after a night shut in the barn as a punishment. Albert Pittet would not fetch the doctor, because the doctor would see the marks of the strap on her back. She died in the farm kitchen on the night of 14 January. Albert's father Joseph had died three days earlier, and his grave was already being dug. On the night before Joseph's funeral, Albert and the gravedigger Raymond Bapst put Rose's body in an apple crate and buried it at the foot of the open grave, deeper than the coffin. Next morning the village buried Joseph on top. Albert told the village that the guardianship office had come for the girl. He told the office nothing and kept cashing her boarding money until March, then reported that she had run away. Nobody searched. Lucien, 11, was told she had gone to hospital in Fribourg. In spring he was moved to another farm.

He spent his life looking for her. At 20 he came back to Leurs and asked too loudly. Albert and the syndic called the gendarmes, and he was locked up for a year at Bellechasse under "administrative internment", the Swiss practice of detaining people without trial that ran until 1981. He learned that asking questions out loud in Leurs gets you locked up.

He worked on farms and in a sawmill. Years in moldy hay as a child left him with farmer's lung, a real allergic lung disease of dairy workers, and by 1998 it has scarred his lungs. Outdoors in the damp he breathes through a filter or he coughs blood. In 1996 Hélène Jaquet, the old village schoolteacher, wrote to him before she died: Rose never left Leurs, she did not know where Rose was, and she was sorry she had stopped asking. Lucien came back in summer 1997 and moved into the disused army fort in the forest. He has been searching ever since: probing the cemetery at night with a steel rod, digging holes across the forest slope, reading every stone.

**Why he hides.** Nobody wants him for a crime. He hides because the last time he was seen in Leurs asking about Rose, the village had him locked away. He is dying and has one thing left to do. Lucien the placed boy would be pitied or sent away. Le Loyon is feared, and fear keeps people out of the forest and off his back.

**Why the mask.** A gas mask from the fort's own store is the best filter he can get, and the doctor told him to wear one. It also hides his face. The cape is an old army tent sheet from the same store. The breath through the filter is his signature sound. The lenses catch a torch and flash for a moment.

**Why the cemetery.** He reads the stones looking for her. He made her a stone because he needed a place to stand. Under it he poured a small concrete vault, empty, waiting for her. He lights the lantern on the teacher's grave, because she was the only one who asked.

Swiss graves are cleared after about 25 years. In 1997 the commune decided to clear the back rows in spring 1999. Germaine Pittet asked to renew her plot and was refused. When it is cleared, Raymond will be the one digging. That is the clock nobody in the village talks about.

## How the player gets in

The fort's command room has no keyhole on the outside. Lucien bolts it from inside. A crank field telephone in the guard room still rings through to the room, because Gilles rewired it for him. On night 3 the reporter rings it. Lucien picks up and only breathes. The reporter says they found Rose. He asks what she had in her hair. The reporter took her ladybird hair clip from the crate. When they describe it, the bolt slides back. The dig is what opens the door: it gives the reporter the one thing Lucien has been looking for.

## Twists, in the order the player meets them

1. The homemade ROSE stone is not a prank. It is a grave for a child who really died here, made by someone who could not find her.
2. The masked man is not hiding a crime of his own. He is searching. The holes on the forest slope look like many graves and turn out to be a year of empty searches.
3. The abbé "buried no Rose", and that is true. But she was in his catechism class, and he wrote one word next to her name.
4. The watchman is not guarding the cemetery for the council. He is guarding a grave he helped fill.
5. Rose is not in her own grave or in any unmarked one. She lies under another man's coffin.
6. Albert went on collecting the boarding money for a dead child.
7. The concrete under the ROSE stone is an empty vault, built by her brother before he found her.

## Endings

Lucien asks the reporter not to write about him. He wants to take Rose out himself, before the spring clearing, and bury her under the stone he made. He no longer trusts any office with her.

- **Publish.** The paper runs the story of the girl who "went back to the city". The canton exhumes and identifies Rose. Nobody can be charged; the time limits ran out long ago. Lucien is taken to hospital in Fribourg. Rose is buried under her own name on 14 January 1999, and Lucien stands at the back with an oxygen bottle and no mask. Without evidence, the editor runs only a short folklore piece about the gas mask man, and in spring the plot is cleared and nothing is found.
- **Keep the secret.** The reporter tells the editor there is nothing. In December the Pittet plot shows fresh earth one morning, and the ROSE stone has new mortar. Raymond says nothing. In spring he clears the plot and finds nothing at the foot. The lantern on the teacher's grave stops being lit in February. Rose lies under her name, and only the reporter and Raymond know.
- **Leave.** The reporter goes home before meeting him. In spring Raymond clears the Pittet plot alone at dawn. The commune breaks up the ROSE stone. The forum keeps reporting the breathing man in the fog.
